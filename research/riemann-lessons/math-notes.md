# Mathematical scope of the retrospective chapter

defines: graph.riemann.lessons.math
consumer: graph.riemann.lessons.script

This chapter distinguishes a proposed rule refuted by a counterexample, an exact identity that does useful arithmetic, and an open computational or analytic obligation. It does not claim that no future zeta-based method could succeed. A visually promising example is not treated as a theorem; a finite implementation test is not used as an all-integer proof.

## Evidence and wording checks

1. **Brush trails:** draw order, overlapping stamps, orientation and resolution affect displayed colors. The chapter does not deny that a separately specified color encoding can preserve arithmetic information. Wilson and fixed-range divisor encodings are discussed explicitly later.
2. **Slope:** the data concern the initial tangent of paths solving ζ(s)=a as real a increases from zero, not arbitrary boundaries of the purple region. At a simple starting zero, ds/da=1/ζ′(s), hence dt/dσ=−Im ζ′/Re ζ′ when the denominator is nonzero. The cohorts are *zero indices*. Among indices1–100, prime indices have14 negative and11 positive slopes; composite indices35 negative and39 positive; index1 is neither and has a negative slope. `Z3` denotes the third positive-height zero, near0.5+25.0108575801i. See `research/zeta-zero-index/README.md` and `proof-notes.md`.
3. **Half-unit substitution:** g(n)=2(n/2−1/2)+2·1/2=n algebraically for all real n. Therefore substituting this expression into the denominator of the zeta Dirichlet series gives Σ_(n≥1)1/g(n)^s=Σ_(n≥1)1/n^s=ζ(s) for Re(s)>1; finite partial sums also agree exactly at every defined s. Its internal half does not set the exponent s or its real part to one half. Substituting the same identity as an argument instead yields ζ(g(n))=ζ(n), which for integer n>1 is positive and greater than1. Neither operation extracts the coefficient indexed by n. For integer inputs, fractional part1/2 after division by2 is shared by all odd integers. The examples97 and99 show that the feature does not distinguish primality. It is not a claim that parity is useless as one step of an algorithm.
4. **Critical line:** ζ(1/2)≈−1.460354509. The family s=1/2+it contains zeros at particular heights, starting near14.134725; not every point is a zero. ζ(1/2+97i)≈1.375849797456+2.047089147780i is a verified counterexample to treating prime heights as zeta zeros. The actual prime connection is collective through established formulas; individual zero indices or heights are not prime labels.
5. **Shifts:** the exact tested rule matches97's quadrant tuple at ζ(s),ζ(s+1),ζ(s−1), where s=1/2+in and2≤n≤100. It retains6 primes and18 composites and misses19 primes. This is not an exhaustive impossibility result for all phase, magnitude or derivative features. The shifts are analytically related, so they are not three independent arithmetic tests. At the unshifted point,97 and99 are both in quadrantI.
6. **Unit switch:**1 remains a unit, not a prime. Removing the additive integer-series term1 gives ζ−1. Its zeros are1-points of ζ and need not lie on the critical line. The product ζ(ζ−1) has the union of these two zero sets away from the pole. A hypothetical off-line zeta zero is retained. No argument establishing RH is obtained by this switch.
7. **Reversal:** two distinct operations are kept separate. Reordering a finite sum leaves its value unchanged. The coefficient reversal R_NF=z^NF(1/z), interpreted polynomially, yields B=F−R_NF with b_(N−n)=−b_n. This cannot be a nonzero nonnegative composite indicator on the full interval0..N. The N10 prime-polynomial control givesz²−z⁸. This does not analyze every possible backward recurrence, masking operation or absolute-value transformation.
8. **Infinite subtraction:** finite whole-number sum minus its prime terms leaves nonprime terms, including1. Knowing that scalar total does not identify each remaining index, and selecting the prime terms already uses prime information. At s=−1 the ordinary Dirichlet series becomes1+2+3+⋯ and diverges; ζ(−1)=−1/12 is its analytic continuation value, not an ordinary convergent sum or a justification for subtracting an infinite prime series.
9. **Actual prime cancellation:** for real s>1, A(s)=(1−2^(−s))ζ(s)>1 and H=A−1−log A have an unambiguous real logarithm. The coefficient in H is0 at odd primes;1−1/k at odd prime powers p^k with k≥2;1 at odd numbers with at least two distinct prime factors;0 at even numbers and at1. In particular the coefficient multiplying97^(−s) is0 while that multiplying99^(−s) is1. H(97)>0. Evaluating an argument does not extract the coefficient bearing that integer index. No efficient extraction procedure was proved by the identity.
10. **Wilson color encoding:** for integers n≥2, R=((n−1)!+1) mod n equals0 exactly at primes. For composites greater than4 R=1; for4 R=3. Therefore F=(1+i)i^R is in quadrantI exactly at primes over this declared domain. The arithmetic supplies the decision, not a pixel. It needs no earlier-prime list but performs modular factorial work. Four-divisor phase rules2,3,5,7 classify the finite range through100 with explicit small-prime/domain handling;121=11² is an outside-range counterexample.
11. **Finite remainder:** D(n) counts nontrivial unordered factor pairs for odd n>1. Hence D(n)=0 iff n is prime on that domain. D(97)=0 and D(99)=2. The elementary all-integer argument is in `research/composite-remainder/proof.md`; independent checks of100001 integer inputs0–100000 are separate evidence about the implementation. D(1)=0 does not make1 prime; the domain guard remains necessary. The through100 classification has25 primes. The construction is divisor counting/trial division or a divisor sieve, not a newly established fast algorithm.
12. **Future work:** for an analytic function nonzero on a closed contour, a rigorous phase-winding calculation can count enclosed zeros (with pole correction for meromorphic functions). A sampled color map alone does not certify a nonvanishing contour, exhaustive count or exact membership on the critical line. A finite zeta verification would still not prove RH for all heights. Finite polynomial collision/gap bounds already in this project do not by themselves transfer to an infinite entire-function evolution. Coefficient extraction needs rigorous error control and cost comparison before efficiency claims.
